# Client Feedback: Northern Bloom Floral & Events

**Website:** https://husson-webdesign.github.io/McBride-P5-Northern-Bloom/

Hello,

Thank you for sharing the first version of our website. The soft pink background, deep green text, floral image, and elegant headings give Northern Bloom a welcoming and polished appearance. I also appreciate that visitors can find our services easily and that choosing Event Planning on the Services page brings them to the contact form with that service already selected.

For the next version, I would like the website to show more of the personality and care behind our work. Someone planning a wedding, ordering an arrangement, or organizing an event should be able to understand what we offer, picture the possibilities, and know what to do next.

## Phase 1: Our Story and Message

### 1. Make our introduction more distinctive

The homepage message, “Beautiful flowers for your most important moments,” is warm and inviting. Please build on it with a short introduction that helps visitors understand what makes Northern Bloom special and the kinds of occasions we serve.

If our location or service area has been provided in the project, make it easy to find. If it has not been provided, add it to your questions for me rather than guessing.

### 2. Give the About page a more personal story

The About page mentions that the business began in 2006 and is committed to quality and memorable experiences. I would like visitors to understand more clearly how we work with customers and what we value when planning their flowers or event.

Please use the project information to develop the story. Ask me to confirm the founding date and any additional details about our team, history, or experience. Do not invent awards, credentials, or customer stories.

### 3. Explain the differences between our services

The Services page introduces General Floral Design, Event Planning, and Custom Floral Design. The descriptions are brief, and a customer might not immediately understand the difference between the two floral design options.

Please give each service a clearer description and examples of occasions it may suit, using approved project information. Help a visitor understand which service to explore without promising a specific product or service that has not been confirmed.

## Phase 2: Visual Design and Presentation

### 4. Build on the existing look

I like the current color palette and the balance between the floral photograph and the introduction. Please carry that same refined look throughout the website.

Use consistent headings, buttons, card styles, and spacing. Keep pages easy to read and avoid making them feel either too crowded or too empty. The site should feel polished, warm, and appropriate for both everyday flowers and special events.

### 5. Show more of the work we offer

The tulip arrangement is attractive, but it appears on both the homepage and About page. Please add a small, thoughtfully arranged gallery or collection of images that helps visitors picture different kinds of floral work and events.

Use images that you have permission to use and document their sources. Do not present a stock photograph as a real customer event or as our own work unless that is accurate. Make sure images fit their spaces and include useful descriptions where appropriate.

### 6. Give visitors a clear next step

Please guide customers through the website. For example:

- From the homepage, visitors can explore the services.
- From the About page, they can see examples or read about the services.
- From each service, they can prepare an inquiry about that service.

The wording on each button should accurately describe what happens when it is selected.

## Phase 3: Navigation and Mobile Experience

### 7. Keep the navigation simple and clear

The current menu is easy to follow, the logo links home, and the current page is visually highlighted. Please preserve these helpful features. I also appreciate the option to skip directly to the main content.

Make sure the menu remains easy to use on smaller screens and that visitors can see where they are when navigating with a keyboard. Any mobile menu should be easy to open and close.

### 8. Make the website comfortable on a phone

Many customers may visit from a phone while planning an event. Please test different screen sizes and make sure:

- Text is readable without zooming.
- Images and service cards fit the screen.
- Buttons and form fields are easy to use.
- Visitors do not need to scroll sideways.
- The navigation and inquiry form remain easy to use.

## Phase 4: Services and Customer Experience

### 9. Make our services easier to explore

Please present each of the three services in a consistent and appealing way. For each one, I would like visitors to see:

- The service name.
- An appropriate image or visual.
- A short explanation of what it involves.
- Examples of suitable occasions, where confirmed.
- A clear way to prepare an inquiry.

If prices, availability, delivery, setup, or timelines have not been provided, please ask for confirmation rather than inventing them.

### 10. Improve the frequently asked questions

The Services page already has a frequently asked questions section, which is a useful addition. Please review the questions and answers to make sure they help customers understand our services and what information to prepare before contacting us.

Make sure the questions can be opened and closed easily using a mouse, touch screen, or keyboard. If answers about delivery, pricing, lead times, or availability have not been confirmed, please identify them as questions for me.

### 11. Make the contact information accurate

The Contact page and footer currently show `+999 (999) 999-9899` and hours written as “Woden's day to Thunor's day at 23:70 JST.” These appear to be placeholders, and the time shown is not valid. Please confirm the correct phone number, operating hours, time zone, and email address against the project brief.

Keep the same approved information across all pages. If the details are fictional for the class project, clearly identify them as sample information. Please do not substitute invented business details or suggest that an address has been verified when it has not.

## Phase 5: Inquiry Form and Interactivity

### 12. Make the inquiry form a clear demonstration

The form asks for useful details, including the customer's name, preferred service, event date, and message. I also like that selecting Event Planning on the Services page brings the visitor to a form where that choice is already selected. Please retain and test this helpful feature for all three services, while allowing the visitor to change the selection.

Because this class project is hosted on GitHub Pages, I understand that the form will **not send or save information**. Please display a clear notice before the form. When a visitor completes it, show a well-organized preview of the answers and allow changes. Do not say that a message was sent or a consultation was booked.

Please rename “Send Message” and similar wording so they describe the demonstration accurately, such as **“Preview Inquiry.”** Make missing information clear and easy to correct.

### 13. Help customers discover a suitable service

Please add a simple way for customers to explore our services based on what they need. For example, they could choose between an arrangement, a customized floral design, and event planning. The result should explain the matching service and offer a way to prepare an inquiry.

Make the feature easy to reset and include a helpful message if no option matches. It should work with a keyboard as well as a mouse or touch screen.

## Phase 6: Make Service Information Easier to Maintain

### 14. Organize service information for future updates

As the business owner, I would like it to be easier for a future developer to update our service descriptions and images. Please keep our service information in a separate **JSON file** and use it to display the three service offerings on the website.

Please make sure:

- General Floral Design, Event Planning, and Custom Floral Design are included.
- Each service has its approved name, description, image information, and inquiry option.
- The information shown on the site matches the information in the file.
- The service discovery feature uses that information.
- Visitors see a helpful message if service information cannot be displayed.
- The project documentation explains where a future developer can find and update the information.

If time permits, you may organize gallery information in a similar way. Please do not add unconfirmed prices or business claims.

## Phase 7: Final Review and Handoff

### 15. Check the finished website

Before presenting the redesign, please review it as though it were ready for customers:

- Check all pages, links, images, buttons, and service selections.
- Make sure the site can be navigated using a keyboard and that the current selection is visible.
- Check that text is readable against its background and images have appropriate descriptions.
- Test phone, tablet, and desktop layouts.
- Confirm that the inquiry form clearly explains it is a demonstration.
- Check that service information loads on the published GitHub Pages website.
- Review the business name, service descriptions, hours, and contact details for consistency.

### 16. Provide a short handoff

Please provide:

- The published website and project repository links.
- A short explanation of the changes you made.
- Screenshots of the website on a desktop and a phone.
- A list of details that still need my confirmation.
- Instructions for a future developer explaining where the service information is kept and how to update it.
- Image sources and any known project limitations.
